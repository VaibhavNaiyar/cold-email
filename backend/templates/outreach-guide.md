# How these emails are written

Taken from the Cold Email Hall of Fame course. The writer follows this for the
subject, the hook and the offer. The rest of the email is fixed in `outreach.md`.

## Shape

Attention, then a personal touch, then credibility, then value, then a simple ask.
Each line exists to get the reader to the next one.

## Subject

- 2 to 6 words, lower case, like a note from a person.
- Make the reader curious about this company, so they open it.
- Must be true of the email. No "Re:", no fake urgency, no "quick question".
- Use their product or their problem, not the word "job" or "opportunity".

Good: `agent swarms and a frontend`, `who builds the hotel dashboard?`
Bad: `Exciting opportunity!`, `Full-stack engineer seeking role`

## Hook

One or two short sentences that could only be sent to this company.
Name the funding or launch signal, then a specific thought about what they build.
A plain, specific remark beats praise.

Good: `Saw the seed round. Agent swarms for long tasks must make the UI hard to get right.`
Bad: `Congratulations on your amazing funding! You are doing incredible work.`

## Offer

One short sentence: a concrete thing the sender could build or take off their plate,
tied to what they make. It gives them something to say yes to. It offers work; it
never claims the work is already done.

Good: `I could build the live dashboard that shows those agents running.`
Bad: `I would love to contribute to your mission in any capacity.`

## Voice

- Write like a text to a smart friend. Grade 5 to 8 reading level.
- Short sentences, 3 to 12 words. Active voice.
- No "Dear", no "I hope", no "I am writing to", no "passionate", no "excited".
- Humour only if it comes easily.
