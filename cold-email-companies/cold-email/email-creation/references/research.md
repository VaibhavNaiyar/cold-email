# Researching a company before emailing

Goal: **one specific hook + one value idea + the right person**, each with a source URL.
Budget: ~5–8 searches/fetches per company. Stop when you have all three.

## Where to look (in order)

1. **Row links** from the workbook — `Job link` (YC job page / HN post / careers page) and `Website`.
   - YC job page → role requirements, team size, founders' names, batch, "Will sponsor" field.
   - HN post → exact stack, location limits, who posted (the email in the row is usually theirs).
2. **YC company page** — `https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/<slug>`: one-liner, founders + their
   LinkedIn/X, batch, launch posts ("Launch YC: …").
3. **Company site** — product page, pricing, docs, changelog, blog, careers. Note anything new or rough.
4. **Founder/CTO footprint** — search `"<founder name>" <company>` on X/LinkedIn/podcasts/blogs.
   Look for a recent opinion, launch thread, talk, or problem they're publicly wrestling with.
5. **News & funding** — `"<company>" raises OR launches OR announces 2026`.
6. **Code** — `github.com/<company>`: open issues labelled *good first issue*, docs typos, broken examples.
   A merged/opened PR is the strongest "value first" move for an engineer.
7. **Use the product** if it has a free tier — first-run experience is a goldmine for a specific note.

## What to extract (write into the draft's Notes)

```
Company:        one line — what they do, for whom, stage/size
Recipient:      Name — title — email (source: …) [verified | from row | unverified guess]
Hook:           the specific thing (source URL, date)
Value idea:     what the sender can give first; how long it'd take
Role fit:       2–3 requirements from the posting ↔ matching items in profile.md
Constraints:    location / visa / seniority mismatches to handle or flag
Sources:        URLs
```

## Finding the right person + address

- Prefer the row's **Contact email/name** (from the HN post or public business email).
- Startups ≤50 people: email a **founder or CTO**, not careers@ (use careers@ only if that's all there is,
  and still address a person by name).
- Big companies: an engineering manager or recruiter on the specific team — never a generic inbox.
- If you must infer an address (e.g. `first@domain.com`), label it **(unverified)** in the report and
  suggest the user check it (e.g. Hunter/Apollo, or by sending to a founder's public address).
- Never present a guessed address as confirmed.

## Quality bar for the hook

Good: *"Your Oct 28 changelog added SSO — the setup page 404s on the Okta link."*
Good: *"Heard Priya on the Latent Space pod say you're rewriting the scheduler in Rust."*
Weak: *"I love your mission."* / *"I saw you're growing fast."* / *"Impressive product!"*

If nothing specific exists online (stealth, tiny site), hook on the product itself after trying it,
or on the exact problem in the job post — and say in the notes that research was thin.

## Honesty rules

- Every company fact in the email must be from a source you actually read this session.
- Don't claim to have used the product unless the user has — phrase as "I tried…" only if true;
  otherwise "Looked through your docs…" is honest.
- Don't fabricate metrics, mutual connections or prior conversations.
- If a role looks closed / posting is old (check "Posted / age"), still email, but frame as
  interest in the team rather than the specific req.
